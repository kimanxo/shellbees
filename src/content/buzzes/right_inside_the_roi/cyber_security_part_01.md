# Cybersecurity Fundamentals

## Overview

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting information, systems, networks, applications, and digital infrastructure from unauthorized access, disruption, manipulation, and destruction. This technical briefing introduces the fundamental principles of cybersecurity, common threats and attack categories, malware families, security controls, identity and access management, cryptography, risk management, and the foundations of defensive security.

## Key Concepts

- **Cybersecurity:** The practice of protecting digital systems, networks, applications, and information from threats.

- **CIA Triad:** The three fundamental goals of information security: Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.

- **Confidentiality:** Ensuring that information is accessible only to authorized individuals or systems.

- **Integrity:** Ensuring that information remains accurate, complete, and protected from unauthorized modification.

- **Availability:** Ensuring that systems and information remain accessible when needed.

- **Asset:** Anything valuable that an organization needs to protect, such as data, systems, applications, or infrastructure.

- **Threat:** A potential event or actor capable of causing harm to an asset.

- **Vulnerability:** A weakness that can potentially be exploited by a threat.

- **Exploit:** A technique, code, or method used to take advantage of a vulnerability.

- **Risk:** The potential for a threat to exploit a vulnerability and cause an undesirable impact.

- **Attack Surface:** The collection of possible entry points through which an attacker could interact with or compromise a system.

- **Attack Vector:** The specific path or technique used to carry out an attack.

- **Malware:** Malicious software designed to disrupt, damage, spy on, control, or gain unauthorized access to systems.

- **Virus:** Malware that attaches itself to legitimate files or programs and typically requires user or system activity to propagate.

- **Worm:** Malware capable of self-propagating across systems or networks without requiring a host file.

- **Trojan:** Malware disguised as legitimate software or files to trick users into executing it.

- **Ransomware:** Malware that typically encrypts or otherwise blocks access to data and demands payment from victims.

- **Spyware:** Malware designed to secretly monitor or collect information from a system or user.

- **Rootkit:** Malware or a collection of techniques designed to hide malicious activity and maintain privileged access.

- **Botnet:** A network of compromised devices controlled by an attacker.

- **Command & Control (C2):** Infrastructure or communication mechanisms used by attackers to control compromised systems.

- **Zero-Day:** A vulnerability that is unknown to the party responsible for fixing it or has not yet received an effective patch.

- **CVE:** A standardized identifier used to publicly reference known cybersecurity vulnerabilities.

- **CVSS:** A standardized scoring system used to communicate the severity of vulnerabilities.

- **Authentication:** Verifying the identity of a user, device, or system.

- **Authorization:** Determining what an authenticated entity is permitted to access or perform.

- **Least Privilege:** Providing users and systems only the permissions required to perform their tasks.

- **Encryption:** Transforming readable information into ciphertext so that it cannot be understood without the appropriate key.

- **Hashing:** Transforming data into a fixed-length value designed primarily for integrity verification and password storage.

- **Digital Signature:** A cryptographic mechanism used to verify the authenticity and integrity of digitally signed information.

- **Firewall:** A security control that filters network traffic according to defined rules.

- **IDS:** An Intrusion Detection System that monitors activity and alerts on suspected malicious behavior.

- **IPS:** An Intrusion Prevention System that can detect and actively block suspected malicious activity.

- **SIEM:** A platform that collects, correlates, and analyzes security-related logs and events.

- **IoC (Indicator of Compromise):** Evidence suggesting that a system may have been compromised.

- **Incident Response:** The structured process of detecting, containing, investigating, and recovering from security incidents.

- **Zero Trust:** A security approach based on continuously verifying access rather than automatically trusting users or devices based on network location.
